# CHM 1045: General Chemistry I

Interactive study guides, practice games, an in-class worksheet, and a virtual lab for a college-level General Chemistry I course. Everything runs in a web browser, with nothing to install and no account to create.

**Students:** these activities are linked from the course in Canvas, and that is the best place to open them, because Canvas shows where each one fits in the week. The list below is here if you want to come back and practice.

## Reactions in Aqueous Solutions Worksheet

A worksheet for the class to work through together in one 60-minute meeting. It has six parts, each with a suggested time, and a timer at the top of the page shows which part the class should be on.

| Part | Topic | What the student does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Electrolytes | Lights a bulb with five solutes, then sorts compounds into strong, weak, and nonelectrolytes |
| 2 | Precipitation reactions | Predicts what forms when two solutions are mixed, then builds a molecular, an ionic, and a net ionic equation |
| 3 | Acid–base reactions | Picks out the strong acids and bases, finds the proton donor, and balances a neutralization |
| 4 | Oxidation–reduction reactions | Assigns oxidation numbers, predicts from the activity series whether a metal reacts, and balances with half-reactions |
| 5 | Concentration and pH | Molarity, dilution, ion concentrations, and pH, with sliders for dilution and the pH scale |
| 6 | Chemical analysis | Identifies an unknown chloride from the mass of a precipitate, and runs a titration to a faint pink endpoint |

- **Every answer is checked on the page.** A hint is always there, and the worked solution opens after a first try. Common slips get a specific pointer, such as a volume left in milliliters or a missed 2:1 ratio.
- **The numbers are the same for everyone,** so the class can work each problem together. Three activities marked "Bonus" are for anyone who finishes early.
- **Work is saved in the student's own browser** and is not sent anywhere. The last tab is a summary that prints, or saves as a PDF, to hand in.
- **For the instructor:** adding `?key` to the address opens every worked solution without a first try, and the "Text size" button enlarges the page for a projector.

## Names & Formulas Lab

A three-part activity for chemical nomenclature: explore a compound and read its name step by step, balance the charges of its ions, then try the challenges. A quick reference at the end lists the common ions, the prefixes, and the acid naming patterns used in the lab.

## Hoth Run

A nomenclature flight trainer. Pilot a snowspeeder through the gate that shows the right answer.

| Sector | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Probe Recon | Ions and charges |
| 2. Ice Field | Binary ionic compounds |
| 3. Polyatomic Ridge | Compounds with polyatomic ions |
| 4. Transition Trench | Metals with Roman numerals |
| 5. Nonmetal Pass | Molecular compounds |
| 6. Generator Flats | Acids and hydrates |
| 7. The Walker (boss) | Mixed review, with typed answers |

- **Steer** with the arrow keys or <kbd>W</kbd> and <kbd>S</kbd>, or press <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>4</kbd> to pick a gate. <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> flies through right away. On a touch screen, tap an answer, then tap it again.
- **Timed or not:** in timed mode the gate comes to you. With the timer off, the speeder waits until you choose.
- **Wrong answers** cost a shield and show why the answer was wrong. Each sector ends with a report of what you missed.
- <kbd>P</kbd> pauses and <kbd>M</kbd> mutes.

Hoth Run is practice only. Your best score for each sector is kept in your own browser and is not sent anywhere.

## The Atomic Spectrum of Hydrogen

A lab on a 3D bench inside a fume hood. Students light a glass tube of hydrogen and find the wavelengths in its light: the lines they can see, read off the scale of a spectroscope, and the ultraviolet and infrared ones, found with a detector. They then work out the energy levels of the hydrogen atom and the wavelength of each jump between levels from Bohr's equation, match every line they measured to a jump, and answer questions about the Balmer series. A second part gives each student an unknown tube to identify by comparing its lines with tubes of helium, neon, argon, krypton, and mercury.

| Part | What the student does |
| --- | --- |
| 1. The light of hydrogen | Measures hydrogen's fifteen lines, four by eye and eleven with the detector |
| 2. The unknown tube | Measures four of its lines and finds the tube that matches |
| 3. Energy levels | Calculates the first ten levels; the page draws the graph |
| 4. Energy changes and wavelengths | Calculates fifteen jumps |
| 5. Which jump gives which line | Matches each measured line to its jump |
| 6. Questions | The Balmer series and its shortest wavelength |

- **Each student has a different unknown.** A five-character code at the top of the page stands for it. Adding `?code=` and a code to the address opens that unknown.
- **The page checks numbers as they are entered** and explains common slips, such as a missing minus sign or a wavelength left in meters. It does not say whether the gas named for the unknown is right: that is for the instructor to mark.
- **Work is saved in the student's own browser** and is not sent anywhere. The last part prints a report, or saves it as a PDF, to hand in.

The lab needs a browser with WebGL, which covers current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari. It works on a phone, but a computer is easier.

### Prelab review

The [prelab review](https://neogio73.github.io/CHM1045/hydrogen-spectrum-lab/prelab/) takes about thirty minutes and is linked from the top of the lab. Nothing in it is graded or recorded.

| Part | What it covers |

| 4. How a spectroscope sorts light | Prism and grating, with practice at reading a scale to a tenth of a nanometer |

| 6. At the bench | What is on the bench, how the page wants numbers typed, and words to know |

Everything the prelab practices on is made up: an imaginary atom with four energy levels, and invented lines to read. It gives away none of the lab's answers.

## Escape from Lewis Manor

A Halloween escape-room game for Chapter 6, Chemical Bonding I. You play a chemistry student locked in a haunted manor at night. Walk the hall with the arrow keys (or the on-screen buttons on a phone), and enter a room with the up arrow. Each room's puzzles break one of the seven wax seals on the front door.

| Room | Topic | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I. The Foyer | The octet rule | 1 |
| II. The Bone Library | Bonds and bond length | 1 |
| III. The Séance Parlor | Electronegativity and polarity | 1 |
| IV. The Crypt | Drawing Lewis structures | 1 |
| V. The Hall of Mirrors | Formal charge | 1 |
| VI. The Phantom Gallery | Resonance | 1 |
| VII. The Tower | Exceptions to the octet rule | 2 |

Each room has three candles. A wrong answer blows one out, and losing all three restarts the room with new questions. The questions change every time. Your browser remembers which seals you have broken, so you can stop and come back with Continue.

Breaking all seven seals ends with a Certificate of Escape: your name, the date, each room's points out of 8, and a verification code. Download it and upload it to the Lewis Manor assignment in Canvas. If you stop early, the Certificate button in the corner gives a Record of Progress for the seals you have broken.

**Instructors:** the "Instructor: check a certificate code" link on the title screen confirms a code. Type the student's name exactly as printed and the code; a valid code shows the score and date it was issued. An edited certificate will not match.

The game has sound, so turn it on. It contains mild, cartoon-style jump scares.

## VSEPR Geometry Explorer

A reference table for every VSEPR shape from 2 to 6 electron groups: all 13 molecular geometries. Each row gives the AXE notation, the number of bonded atoms and lone pairs, the ideal and real bond angles, the polarity, and a note on why the shape looks the way it does. A heading for each electron-group count gives the electron geometry, ideal angles, and hybridization. A short primer at the top covers predicting a shape, what counts as one electron group, why lone pairs shrink angles, and how to tell polarity from shape.

- **Every row has a 3D model** to drag and rotate, with lone pairs drawn as purple lobes and the bond angle marked on the model.
- **A menu above each model switches between example molecules,** 49 in all, such as H₂O, H₂S, OF₂, and SCl₂ for the bent shape. Each example lists its central atom, its electron groups, its measured bond angle, and whether it is polar.
- **Switches at the top** turn the lone pairs, angle labels, and rotation on or off.

Nothing on the page is graded or recorded. The models need a browser with WebGL.

## For instructors

### How it is hosted

The repository is published with GitHub Pages from the `main` branch, so every file is served at `https://neogio73.github.io/CHM1045/` followed by its path. A change merged into `main` goes live a minute or two later.

Canvas links point at these exact addresses. Renaming or moving a file breaks its link in Canvas, which is also why the `vsepr-chamber-06` folder keeps its name even though it now holds chambers 02 to 06.

### Editing the activities

- **Study guides, the worksheet, Names & Formulas Lab, and Hoth Run** are each a single HTML file with the styles, script, and images inside it. They load nothing from other sites.
- **VSEPR Geometry Explorer** is a single file, [`vsepr-geometry-explorer.html`](vsepr-geometry-explorer.html). Its table and example molecules are in the `DATA` list near the top of its script. It loads three.js from a public CDN and its fonts from Google Fonts.
- **Escape from Lewis Manor** is also a single file, [`lewis-manor/index.html`](lewis-manor/index.html), with its art and sound made in code. It loads its three fonts from Google Fonts and falls back to built-in fonts without them.
- **VSEPR Test Chambers** keeps the content an instructor is likely to change in [`vsepr-chamber-06/js/content.js`](vsepr-chamber-06/js/content.js): the chambers, molecules, questions, and narrator lines. Adding `?chamber=2` through `?chamber=6` to the address opens one chamber directly, and `?chamber=menu` opens the list. With nothing added, chamber 06 opens.
- **The Atomic Spectrum of Hydrogen** is a finished build of a 3D project that is kept outside this repository. Its folder is replaced as a whole when the lab changes, so edits made to its files here would be lost.

### Running a copy on your own computer

Download or clone the repository. The study guides, the worksheet, the Names & Formulas Lab, Hoth Run, Escape from Lewis Manor, and the VSEPR Geometry Explorer open straight from the file with a double-click.

The VSEPR chambers and The Atomic Spectrum of Hydrogen have to be served by a web server, because browsers will not load their scripts and 3D files from a file on disk. From the repository folder, run:

```bash
python -m http.server 8000
```

Then open `http://localhost:8000/vsepr-chamber-06/` or `http://localhost:8000/hydrogen-spectrum-lab/`.
